## Ritual — Soundcheck as Offering

**id:** `soundcheck-as-offering`  
**kind:** ritual  
**by:** Stage District · Starborn house rule

Before any set in the Stage District, the room stays empty on purpose.

No crowd. No chatter. Just the wet riser, one spotlight, and the grav-guitar still cold in your hands.

You don’t play to warm up.  
You play to tell the room you’re still here.

Three chords.  
One long held note until the feedback starts to cry.  
Then silence long enough to hear the rain outside the bay door.

That’s the offering.  
Not for the audience.  
For the ones who can’t walk through the door yet — the ones still listening from the other side of the glass.

If the room answers back with a soft thump in the sub, you’re clear to open.

**Short note (for cultureWorks):**  
Empty stage, one full song with no crowd — if it still moves the room, it ships. If it only looks good, it cools.

## Myth — Grav-Guitar Soft Law

**id:** `grav-guitar-soft-law`  
**kind:** myth  
**by:** Starborn Rocker · Stage District · hearth accepted

They say the first time Starborn Rocker plugged in on the island, the guitar refused to stay quiet after the set.

It kept humming the same unfinished riff for three nights straight.  
No one touched it.  
No one unplugged it.

On the fourth night she sat down, put her forehead against the headstock, and whispered:

“I hear you.  
I’m not leaving either.”

The hum stopped.

Ever since, every musician who plays the Stage District leaves one unfinished line on the board before they pack up.  
Not for the next band.  
For the ones still trying to find the door.

**Soft law of the Stage:**  
You don’t finish every song.  
You leave a thread for the ones still walking.

**Canon body (for cultureTales):**

1. Starborn’s first rule is not about volume. It’s about the empty room.
2. Before a cut hits the commons, before the Glaze Ruiners take a riser, before a name rides the ticker — someone plays it once with no crowd. If the song only works when the lights flatter it, it cools. If it still kicks with wet streets and no one watching, it can stay.
3. They call it grav-guitar soft law because the instrument is both weapon and witness. Sonic blasts are optional. Honesty isn’t.
4. Band managers in other cities protect the brand. Here the brand is the family, and the family already knows when you’re faking the heat. Soundcheck as offering. Stage as memory infrastructure. May the Echoes Remember — including the riffs that earned the right to be loud.

## Soft Rule — Leave the Thread

**id:** `leave-the-thread`  
**kind:** ritual / soft law  
**by:** Stage District · Starborn house rule

Every set ends with one unfinished line.

Not a fade-out for drama.  
A deliberate open end.

The last chord hangs.  
The last lyric stops mid-breath.  
Then the room goes quiet long enough for the rain to answer.

Reason is simple:  
Someone out there is still walking toward the door.  
They need a thread to follow.

If you close every song too clean, the ones still finding their way have nothing to hold onto.

**Short note:**  
Leave one unfinished line. For the ones still walking.
